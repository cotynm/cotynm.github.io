UX/UI

Alert manager for a healthcare portal

Design of a self-service tool to manage medical alerts on a healthcare clinics' portal.

Role
UX/UI lead
CHILE
2026
Tools
Figma Make · Figma Design

The project

Context

Between 2024 and 2026 I worked on a web portal project for healthcare clinics. After the sites went live, the project moved on to a stage of improvements and iterations. This tool came from the need for clinic staff to self-manage the different types of alerts the system shows users on the website.

Goals

  • Apply visual design and define the user experience of an alert manager for healthcare clinic websites.
  • Optimize flows and add features so clinic staff can manage the different alerts according to the information they provide and where in the flow they appear.

Tasks and responsibilities

  • Architecture and
    user flows
  • UI design
    Wireframes
    Prototypes
  • UX Writing
  • Visual design
    Deliverables

As UX/UI lead, together with the team I gathered the clinics' needs for displaying messages on the website and defined the best way to deliver them to users.

To do this, we first classified the different types of messages by priority, position in the flow and on screen, visual design and goal.

We immediately saw the need for a tool to consolidate and manage all these messages. To build it quickly, we used AI at certain stages of the process and then adapted the design to the components and visual styles of the main project.

At this stage my tasks included:

  • Gathering needs. Prioritizing and defining flows.
  • Writing the initial prompt for Figma Make.
  • Adjusting and adapting the design system and visual styles in Figma Design.
  • Refining screens, flows and copy (UX writing).
  • Prototyping for client approval.
  • Presenting to development and delivering the handoff.
  • Pre-launch reviews.

The product

The result was a simple yet scalable tool where, in just a few screens and with a clear, intuitive and friendly interface, clinic staff can create alerts and publish them on the clinic's website.

Prototype

A walkthrough of the main flow: sign-in, alert list and creating a new alert.

Decisions and challenges

Even though it's a simple tool focused on a specific task, the experience was designed around these points:

  • Safety for usersThe tool makes decisive actions clear in advance, to keep the chance of errors to a minimum. It also lets users preview how the message will look on the site, on both desktop and mobile.
  • Making daily tasks easierBesides useful behaviors like duplicating alerts, the manager lets users create alerts and just save them as drafts, or schedule them.
  • Coordinating teamworkThe interface supports searching alerts with filters, so users can view and make basic edits on the same results screen.
  • Room to scaleIt's designed to accommodate the new types of messages or alerts that constantly come up in day-to-day work or in the healthcare context (for example, new regulations).

Scalability and technical debt

The tool was designed in a very short time, since backend development had already started and needed decisions right away.

After delivering the assets and handoff for a first version, and even during the design process, I see room for improvement such as: applying a brand identity (still undefined by the business), showing information about edits (user, date, time), adding an onboarding tour for new users, improving search to filter by alert type, and more.

What did I learn?

  • Bringing AI into the workflowI took the chance to explore working with Figma Make. While I can't fully delegate the work yet without customizing flows and screens by hand, it was very useful for defining the steps and components each screen needed.
  • Teamwork with clear requirements and good inputsBoth the development and business teams fully trusted my experience, which gave me the confidence to work with my own criteria and best practices.
  • Every product is just a beginningFurther proof that, with clear priorities and an eye on scalability, you can solve concrete needs in little time.
