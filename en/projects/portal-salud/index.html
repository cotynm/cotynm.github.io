UX/UI

Web portal ecosystem for healthcare

End-to-end redesign of a group of clinics' web ecosystem, creating a consistent, secure and patient-centered experience.

Role
UX lead
CHILE
2024 – 2026
Scope
5 websites unified into one portal

The project

Context

The company owns a group of healthcare clinics, each with its own website. Over the years, these sites grew by adding features as needs arose, which made them operationally useful but inconsistent in structure, navigation and experience.

Information was disorganized, flows varied from clinic to clinic, and medical booking —a critical process— had friction. The challenge was to organize, unify and optimize from a user-centered perspective without losing features that were key for the business.

Goals

UX

  • Unify navigation patterns across clinics.
  • Reduce friction and cognitive load by simplifying flows.
  • Organize and prioritize clinical information.
  • Optimize for diverse profiles, including older adults.

UI

  • Build visual consistency across clinics while respecting each identity.
  • Create style guides and a library of reusable components.
  • Set scalable guidelines for future features.

As the project's UX lead, I was responsible for the portal's end-to-end experience:

  • Information architecture.
  • Flow design and optimization (with a special focus on medical booking).
  • Wireframes and prototypes.
  • High-fidelity UI design.
  • Design system development (ongoing).
  • UX writing and microcopy.
  • Functional QA and pre- and post-production review.
  • Post-launch iterations and continuous improvements.

I worked with different teams: core portal, content, improvements squad and QA. Every task was coordinated with the development, legal and security, SEO and infrastructure areas.

I led meetings with stakeholders to gather needs, present design solutions and coordinate adjustments with other teams.

The product

Features and functionality

What do users need to get done?

  • Book a medical appointment.
  • Access their private portal to manage their tests, appointments, account, etc.
  • Learn about the clinic and find general health information.

What does the business need?

  • Offer patients a platform where they can complete procedures, see their history and manage private information, reducing offline inquiries.
  • Have an institutional space to centralize information, communicate its expertise and build its position in the healthcare field.
  • Promote products and benefits.

Visual design

I started out working with flows and a few high-fidelity wireframes, so I had to create the screens for the complete flows and the components, and define the style guides for the whole project.

Service delivery and handoff

Solutions were constantly shaped by how the services deliver information.

So, before working on any design, I reviewed the data input and asked about any limitations that might exist.

At the same time, a clean, coordinated and thorough handoff to development was essential, so that the result would be as faithful as possible to the proposal approved by the business.

Challenges

There were many! Still, these were the most interesting ones:

  • Security, sensitive data and regulatory complianceEvery decision must meet security standards. Deployments go through external audits and strict controls before each release to production. We also work closely with the clinics' legal teams.
  • Working with multiple stakeholdersBalancing priorities, teaching UX criteria, negotiating decisions and aligning expectations was a core part of the project. Even so, I really enjoyed the presentations to the business, where I defended the decisions made and we gathered feedback.
  • Not losing existing featuresMany internal tools were valuable, even if they weren't optimized for UX. The challenge was to rethink them with the resources available and reach an improved proposal that the clinics' staff, already used to them, would accept.

Results

Joint work between design, development, security and QA made it possible to build a powerful, versatile tool for the business, and a useful, complete platform for each clinic's patients.

  • A consistent experience across clinics.
  • Clearer, more structured navigation.
  • A simpler, clearer booking flow.
  • Self-managed sections for dynamic updates.
  • Stronger perception of professionalism and brand solidity.
  • A scalable foundation for adding new features.

From the first days after each site went live, there was an increase in medical bookings, according to data analysis, internal feedback and operational perception.

What did I learn?

Besides everything that comes with being part of a healthcare project, I'd highlight:

  • Building good day-to-day relationshipsTeamwork isn't only about the company's internal teams. Stakeholders have a lot of knowledge and value to bring, and building a healthy, collaborative relationship is key for the project.
  • Getting something good out of lost battlesOften, like it or not, business interests outweigh user needs. Still, there's (almost) always something we can do to soften the impact: a shortcut, a piece of microcopy, a hint or a subtle design resource.
  • Learning to let goI learned to set aside designs that had come together perfectly in favor of iterations requested by the client. When the new proposal is better, it's easier.
