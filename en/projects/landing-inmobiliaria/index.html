Sales landing page for a real estate development

Strategy, UX/UI and content for a landing page that brings together all the information about Módulo 5 and turns inquiries into sales leads.

Role
End-to-end UX/UI designer
ARG
2026
Tools
Figma Make · Figma Design

The project

Context

Módulo 5 is a set of five duplexes in Córdoba, designed both for people looking for a place to live and for investors. With campaigns running, inquiries came in through WhatsApp (“send me info”, “send me something about the project”) and there was no single piece to centralize the information and support sales.

There was already a landing page, but it had been built without a UX or sales perspective, and it followed the real estate agency's brand rather than the project's.

Goals

  • Centralize the project information in a single piece that supports the Meta and Google campaigns and works as a sales tool in conversations with clients.
  • Achieve a high-quality, refined and simple structure and design that is also appealing, so people can get to know the product in a clear, enjoyable way.
  • Present Módulo 5 as a residential complex designed for a different way of living, centering its value on the independence between units and its quality architecture, not as just another housing development.
  • Give the client a reusable tool or template for future projects to market, which they can also offer as added value in their sales proposal.

Challenges

  • Lead captureThe client had a basic HubSpot license, but no qualified staff or extra permissions to build the page there. Since managing leads was essential, I proposed creating the form in HubSpot and embedding it in a custom-built landing page.
  • No brand guidelines and limited resourcesThere was only a one-color logo, renders and construction photos. I defined the palette, iconography and visual style for this development: Módulo 5. This visual framework also worked for other pieces, such as the street sign.
  • Curate, organize and prioritizeInstead of dumping the full technical specifications, I reorganized the information to prioritize a visual, conceptual experience focused on conversion.

Color palette

Starting from the one-color logo and the project's materials (exposed concrete, wood, earth tones), I defined a warm, understated palette in line with the contemporary, architectural and minimalist tone the client was looking for.

The product

Design decisions

  • First, what draws people inThe image and the concept open the page: visual impact, key differentiators and a quick way to inquire. Features and technical information come later, with visual cues for quick scanning.
  • Contact, always at handA floating WhatsApp button, a “Get information” call to action fixed in the menu and a short form, so no one gets lost along the way.
  • Fast, controlled navigationA menu that follows the scroll lets people move around as they need and reach each section clearly, with features that ensure control, reversibility and an easy way back to where they started.
  • A form that helps sellFew required fields and a multiple-choice purchase method, so the sales advisor starts the conversation with context.
  • Confidence at every stepConstruction progress with real photos and the estimated handover date answer the most common questions before they come up.
  • A voice of its ownContemporary, warm copy, with no pushy real estate language, in line with the agency’s tone of voice, focused on closeness and trust.

Bonus: street sign

For the façade I designed a sign that takes the project’s identity to the street, built to be read in a fast-moving, passing context: a large, striking photo, big clear lettering, generous spacing and minimal text. It includes a QR code that opens the landing page for more information.

What did I learn?

  • Advising is also designingProposing the right technical solution and coordinating with the development team on the best way to implement it was key to making the project viable with the resources the client had.
  • There is no design without contentCurating and writing the information mattered as much as the visual design for the landing page to work as a sales tool and become a scalable, reusable product.
